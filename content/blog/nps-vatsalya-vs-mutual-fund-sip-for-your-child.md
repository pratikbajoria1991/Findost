If you're a parent, you've probably seen NPS Vatsalya mentioned everywhere this week. New rules on withdrawals and exits just kicked in, and the pitch sounds great: start young, let compounding do the work, get a tax deduction along the way.

But is it actually the best place to park money for your child? Or is a simple mutual fund SIP the smarter move? Here's a plain-language breakdown.

## What NPS Vatsalya actually is

It's a National Pension System account opened in your child's name, run by you until they turn 18.

- Minimum contribution: **₹1,000 a year**. No upper limit.
- Money is invested NPS-style, with up to **75% in equity** if you choose the aggressive option.
- At 18, the account converts into your child's own regular NPS account, or they can exit.

That last point is the part most people miss: **this is fundamentally a retirement account**, just one that starts absurdly early.

## The catch: your money is genuinely stuck

Before 18, you can only withdraw **25% of your own contributions**, and only after a 3-year lock-in, and only for specific reasons — the child's education, a serious illness, or disability.

At 18, what happens depends on the corpus:

- Below **₹2.5 lakh** → your child can withdraw the whole thing.
- **₹2.5 lakh to ₹8 lakh** → at least 80% must buy an annuity (a pension product); only 20% comes out as cash.
- Above **₹8 lakh**, exiting between 18-21 → the annuity requirement drops to just 20%, so up to 80% can be withdrawn.

If your child doesn't exit, the account simply continues as their personal NPS — locked up till they're 60. So if your actual goal is funding college or a wedding at 21 or 25, a big chunk of a well-grown Vatsalya account may not be freely usable exactly when you need it.

## The tax deduction is smaller than it sounds

Contributions get you up to **₹50,000** extra deduction under Section 80CCD(1B), on top of the regular 80C limit. Sounds good — except this only works in the **old tax regime**. Most salaried taxpayers have already shifted to the new regime, where this deduction simply doesn't apply. Worth checking before you assume you're saving tax.

## The simpler alternative: a mutual fund SIP

An equity mutual fund SIP started in your name (earmarked mentally, or via a folio for the child) gives you:

- **Full liquidity** — no lock-in, no withdrawal reasons to justify.
- Flexibility to redeem for school fees at 10, a laptop at 16, or college at 18 — whatever comes first.
- Taxation you already understand: **12.5% LTCG** above **₹1.25 lakh** a year, **20% STCG** below a year.

For a **daughter specifically**, Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana is also worth comparing — currently **8.2%**, tax-free, government-backed, though debt-only and locked till she's 21 or marries after 18.

## So which one wins?

Neither, outright — they solve different problems.

- Money you'll need for **school, college, or a wedding** in the next 10-20 years → a mutual fund SIP (or SSY for a daughter) is more useful because you can actually access it when the goal arrives.
- Money you're happy to lock away as a genuine **head-start on your child's own retirement** → NPS Vatsalya's early compounding and forced discipline are a real advantage, precisely because you can't touch it.

A reasonable approach: keep your core child-goal money in SIPs where you control the timing, and treat NPS Vatsalya as a small, optional add-on — not the main plan.
