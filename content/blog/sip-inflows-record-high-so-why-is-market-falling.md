If you've checked your mutual fund app lately and felt confused, you're not alone. Every news channel is saying SIP inflows just hit a **record ₹31,781 crore in June 2026** — the fifth straight month above the ₹31,000 crore mark. And yet the Nifty 50 is down close to **10% from its January 2026 high**. Your monthly SIP is bigger than ever, but your portfolio isn't exactly cheering.

This confuses a lot of first-time investors, because it feels like it shouldn't add up. Here's what's actually happening.

## Two very different sets of buyers and sellers

- **Foreign institutional investors (FIIs)** have pulled out close to **₹1.9 lakh crore** from Indian equities in the first few months of 2026 — driven by rising crude oil prices, a weaker rupee, global interest rate moves, and geopolitical tension, not by anything wrong with Indian companies specifically.
- **Domestic institutional investors (DIIs)** — largely mutual funds, powered by your SIP money — have bought back almost **₹1.7 lakh crore** in the same period, absorbing roughly 90% of that foreign selling.

For the first time, domestic institutions now **own more of Indian markets (18.9%) than FIIs do (14.7%)**. That's a genuine structural shift. But it also means one thing clearly: your SIP money isn't pushing the market up right now — it's cushioning the fall.

## Why "record inflows" doesn't mean "market goes up"

This is the part that trips people up. SIP flows are a **floor, not a lever**. They stop the market from crashing when foreign money exits; they don't guarantee the index will rally, especially when the selling pressure is this large and global in origin. Markets move on the balance between buyers and sellers, and right now that balance is roughly even — not tilted enough either way for a clear trend.

## What this actually means for your SIP

1. **Your SIP is buying more units at lower prices right now.** That's not a bug — it's the entire point of rupee-cost averaging. A flat or falling market for a few quarters, while your SIP keeps running, is quietly good for your long-term average cost.
2. **Don't confuse a paper loss with a real one.** If your goal is 5-10 years away, a 10% index dip along the way is normal, not a red flag.
3. **Check your asset mix, not just your equity fund.** If a 10% equity fall is making you anxious, your equity allocation may simply be too aggressive for your risk appetite — that's worth fixing regardless of what the market does next.
4. **Avoid switching a lump sum in at "the bottom."** Nobody — including fund managers — reliably knows where that is. If you have a large lump sum to deploy, spreading it via an STP over 6-12 months is usually the calmer choice.
5. **Resist the urge to redeem in panic.** Selling equity mutual fund units within 12 months attracts **20% STCG**; beyond 12 months, gains above **₹1.25 lakh a year** are taxed at **12.5% LTCG**. Selling in a dip locks in the tax hit along with the loss.

FII selling and DII buying will keep pulling in opposite directions for a while — that's a feature of India's markets maturing, not a warning sign to exit. The investors who tend to do well are the ones who keep the SIP running and stop checking the NAV every day.
