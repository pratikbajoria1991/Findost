If your salary slip looks a little thinner this year, you're not imagining it. Since the **four new labour codes** took effect (from November 2025, with final central rules notified in May 2026), many companies have been quietly restructuring pay - and for a lot of salaried Indians, that means a smaller number hitting the bank account every month.

This isn't a pay cut. It's a rule change. Here's what's actually happening and what to do about it.

## What changed

The Code on Wages now requires that **Basic Pay + Dearness Allowance + retaining allowance add up to at least 50% of your total CTC**. Earlier, many companies kept basic pay low (30-40% of CTC) and loaded the rest into allowances like special pay, which are only partly counted for statutory calculations.

Now, if your basic was below 50%, your employer has to top it up - and that has knock-on effects:

- **PF contribution goes up.** Both employee and employer PF (12% each) are calculated on basic + DA. A higher basic means a bigger PF deduction every month.
- **Gratuity accrual goes up.** Gratuity is based on last-drawn wages (roughly basic × 15/26 per year of service), so this rises too.
- **Take-home falls.** More of your CTC is going into PF and gratuity, both of which you can't touch until retirement or resignation. Depending on your salary structure, in-hand pay can drop by roughly **2-5%**, though some employers are raising CTC to soften the blow.

A simplified example: someone whose take-home used to be ₹2,000 higher a month might now see that ₹2,000 disappear - but their PF contribution rises by roughly ₹1,000/month and gratuity accrual by a few hundred rupees a month. Your numbers will differ; this is illustrative, not a formula to copy blindly.

## What this means for you as an investor

The money isn't lost - it's just been moved from "spendable now" to "locked-in retirement savings" (EPF) and "long-service benefit" (gratuity). That's not necessarily bad. But it does mean three things worth checking:

1. **Recheck your monthly budget before touching your SIPs.** If take-home has dropped, don't reflexively pause your SIP - first see if there's slack elsewhere (subscriptions, discretionary spends). Your SIP is usually the last thing that should get cut, not the first.

2. **Treat the extra PF as part of your debt allocation, not a bonus.** EPF is a debt-like, government-backed instrument. If your overall asset allocation plan already accounts for a debt component, a bigger forced EPF contribution can mean you need *less* separate debt fund investing to hit the same equity:debt ratio - which frees up room for equity SIPs elsewhere.

3. **Re-verify your tax regime choice.** A higher basic pay changes your HRA exemption calculation (HRA is usually a % of basic) and your total taxable income under the old regime. If you're on the old regime for 80C/HRA benefits, it's worth recomputing which regime saves you more this year - don't assume last year's choice still holds.

4. **If take-home has genuinely shrunk, rebuild your emergency fund math.** Your target of 6 months' expenses might now need recalculating against a lower monthly cash inflow.
