If you've tried to buy a Sovereign Gold Bond recently and come up empty, you're not imagining it. The government hasn't opened a fresh SGB tranche since **February 2024**, and there's no issuance calendar for this year either — high gold prices and high borrowing costs have made SGBs an expensive product for the government to keep offering.

For over a decade, SGBs were the gold standard (literally) for Indian investors: no GST, no locker risk, an extra **2.5% annual interest**, and fully tax-free capital gains if you held to the 8-year maturity. Naturally, people are asking: **now what?**

## What you can still do with SGBs

- **You can't subscribe to new bonds** — that window is shut for now, with no signal it's reopening soon.
- **You can buy or sell existing SGBs on NSE/BSE** through your demat account, just like a stock.
- **If you already own SGBs**, you can simply hold them to maturity as planned.

Here's the part that changed in 2026 and trips people up: a Budget 2026 amendment to Section 70(1)(x) of the Income-tax Act now limits the capital-gains exemption at maturity to the **original subscriber who holds continuously till the full 8-year term**. If you buy someone else's SGB on the exchange midway, you no longer get that tax-free treatment the original allottee enjoyed — your gains get taxed like any other listed bond. So secondary-market SGBs aren't the tax-free shortcut they used to look like.

## So where should new gold money go?

For pure investment (not jewellery you plan to wear), here's how the alternatives stack up on tax:

1. **Gold ETFs** — the most tax-efficient option now. Listed on the exchange, **12-month** holding for long-term gains, taxed at a flat **12.5%** with no indexation. No GST on purchase, easy to buy in small amounts, no locker needed.
2. **Gold mutual funds (including SIPs)** — great if you want to automate small monthly amounts without a demat account. Taxed the same **12.5%**, but you need to hold **24 months** to qualify as long-term; gains before that are taxed at your slab rate.
3. **Digital gold** — convenient for very small, spontaneous purchases, but it's **not SEBI-regulated**, spreads (buy-sell price gaps) can be wide, and it carries the same 24-month rule as physical gold. Treat it as a top-up, not your core gold holding.
4. **Physical gold/jewellery** — carries **3% GST** on purchase plus making charges, storage and purity concerns, and the same 24-month LTCG holding period. Fine for occasions and tradition, inefficient as an investment.

Notice none of these give you the old SGB combination of tax-free maturity gains plus 2.5% extra interest. That specific deal is gone for new investors — the closest substitute for disciplined, low-cost gold exposure today is a **Gold ETF**, or a **gold mutual fund SIP** if you'd rather not open a separate demat/trading setup.

## One reminder on "how much"

This isn't a call to go big on gold — most advisors still suggest keeping gold to a modest slice of a well-diversified portfolio, sized to your goals, not to headlines about gold prices. If you haven't worked out your right allocation, that's worth doing before picking a product.

None of this is a promise of returns — gold prices, like any market-linked asset, can be volatile, and past performance doesn't guarantee what happens next.
