---

If you renewed your health insurance recently and felt confused seeing a bigger number, you're not imagining it.

Since **22 September 2025**, individual health insurance policies in India — including senior citizen plans and family floaters — attract **0% GST**, down from 18%. On paper, that should have made every premium cheaper by roughly a sixth. For a lot of people, it didn't work out that way.

## Where did the saving go?

GST and the **base premium** are two separate things. The tax rate dropped, but insurers reset base premiums at the same time — and that reset had nothing to do with GST.

The real driver is **medical inflation**, which insurers say is running at **13-15% a year** in India right now. Hospital bills, diagnostics, and implant costs keep climbing, so insurers reprice policies to keep pace with the claims they expect to pay. Add an ageing policyholder base and richer plan features, and the base premium hike can easily be bigger than the GST saving.

The net effect, seen across several renewals this year: a family floater that cost roughly **₹40,000** a year before September 2025 is now landing closer to **₹45,000** — even with zero tax on top.

One safeguard worth knowing: **IRDAI caps the annual premium hike on senior citizen health policies at 10%** unless the insurer gets prior regulatory approval. If your parents' renewal jumped by more than that in one go, it's worth asking the insurer to explain the approval behind it.

## What to actually do about it

- **Don't auto-renew without comparing.** Health insurance portability rules let you switch insurers at renewal without losing your accumulated waiting-period credit for pre-existing conditions. A five-minute comparison can be worth thousands.
- **Split parents from the family floater.** Most family floaters stop covering members after a certain age (often 65), and pricing a senior citizen inside a floater is usually costlier than a dedicated senior-citizen policy bought separately.
- **Size the cover to medical inflation, not old habits.** A **₹5 lakh** cover that felt adequate a decade ago is thin today. For parents, **₹15-25 lakh** is a more realistic starting point in most cities, higher if you're in a metro.
- **Use a super top-up instead of over-buying a base plan.** A large base policy is expensive; a modest base plus a super top-up (which kicks in after a deductible) usually gets you a bigger effective cover for a smaller extra premium.
- **Claim what Section 80D still allows** — but only if you're on the old tax regime. You can claim up to **₹25,000** for premiums on yourself and family (**₹50,000** if you're a senior citizen), plus another **₹25,000** for parents' premiums (**₹50,000** if they're senior citizens), with a **₹5,000** sub-limit for preventive health check-ups inside that cap. On the new regime, none of this applies — so factor that into your regime choice, not just your salary structure.

Health insurance isn't an investment, but a bad renewal decision can wreck an otherwise well-planned portfolio the day a hospital bill lands. It's worth the ten minutes it takes to actually compare.
