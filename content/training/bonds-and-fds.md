Bonds are **loans you give to a company or government — they pay you fixed interest (coupon) and return your money at maturity**. They sit between savings accounts and equity: steadier than shares, better-earning than savings.

**Reading a bond like a pro (3 things only):**

1. **Credit rating** — AAA = highest safety; AA = strong; A and below = meaningfully higher risk. Higher interest almost always means higher risk, never a free lunch.
2. **Yield (XIRR)** — your actual return if you buy today and hold to maturity. This matters more than the printed coupon.
3. **Secured vs unsecured** — secured bonds have specific assets backing them if things go wrong.

**What the market currently offers (indicative, via Findost's Wealthy desk):**

- **AAA corporate bonds** (HDFC Bank, Bajaj Finance, M&M, REC): ~**7.3–7.8% XIRR** — the high-safety zone.
- **Government-guaranteed / state bonds** (e.g. KIIFB, state corporations): ~**8–8.6% XIRR** with quarterly payouts.
- **High-yield private NCDs**: **9–11%+** — only for a small, risk-aware slice of your portfolio.
- **Corporate FDs** (Bajaj Finance, Shriram Finance): simple fixed deposits at rates above bank FDs; check the issuer's rating first.
- **54EC capital-gains bonds** (REC/PFC/IRFC) — a special tax-saving category, ask me about them separately.

**Taxation:** bond interest and corporate-FD interest are taxed at your slab rate.
